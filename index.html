Find Your Main Water Shutoff Valve — Before You Need It

When a pipe bursts, one step matters more than everything else combined: shutting off the main water valve. It stops the damage from getting worse while you think. The worst time to go looking for it is with water spreading across the floor, so here is where it hides in Brighton and Denver-metro homes.

Start on the street-facing side

In most local homes, the main shutoff sits on the street-facing side of the house, close to where the water line comes through the foundation wall. Check the basement first, then the crawlspace, utility room, or garage. Builders keep it near the entry point of the line, so follow the pipe, not the fixtures.

Two valve types, two motions

A round wheel is a gate valve: turn it clockwise until it stops. A lever handle is a ball valve: it is off when the lever sits across the pipe, at a right angle to the flow. If the wheel is stiff, do not force it with a wrench mid-emergency — note it and move to the backup below.

The backup: the curb stop valve

No indoor valve, or it will not budge? The backup is the curb stop at the property line: a round lid in the ground, often stamped "WATER." Opening it usually takes a meter key, which is exactly why finding your indoor valve on a calm day is worth ten minutes.

After the water is off

Open cold faucets first, then hot, to drain the lines and take pressure off the break. Photograph the burst area, the water trail, and every damaged surface — insurers draw a hard line between sudden damage, which standard policies usually cover, and gradual leaks, which they usually do not. Your photos are the proof.
